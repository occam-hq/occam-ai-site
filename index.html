What we build

Three ways we take work off your plate

Automations

Work that runs itself

The tasks your team repeats every day, handled automatically by rules you set. Anything unusual goes to a person to decide.

Integrations

Every part, connected

Whether it’s a tool we build or an outside one worth keeping, every part of the system works together, and information moves through it without anyone retyping it. Where a tool holds you back, we replace it with one you own.

Platforms

One place to run it all

A custom platform built around how your business works, where your team sees every client, task, and decision in one place.

Entities should not be multiplied beyond necessity. Occam’s razor, after William of Ockham. We build the same way: no more moving parts than the problem demands.

How we work

From a short call to a running system

  1. Step 1

    A 15-minute call

    Tell us where your team’s time goes. We’ll tell you plainly whether a system can take that work over, and what it would take.

  2. Step 2

    We build it for you

    We design it around how your business works, keeping the tools that serve you and replacing the ones that don’t. No templates, and no parts the problem doesn’t need. It runs in accounts you own.

  3. Step 3

    We run and refine it

    After launch we keep it running, watch the results, and adjust it as your business changes. Every action it takes is logged, and you can switch it off at any time.

About us

Two founders, on every project

AI can now do a lot of the work that fills a small business’s day. But most owners are too busy running the business to work out where it fits, and hiring engineers to build it is out of reach.

We started Occam AI to close that gap. Both of us get to know your business first: how the work moves, where the time goes, and what a system could take over. Then Mayer leads the build, and Joseph makes sure it stays true to what you need. You work directly with the two of us, before launch and after.

The name comes from Occam’s razor: the simplest solution is usually the best one. Simple systems are cheaper to build, easier to trust, and less likely to break, so that is what we build.
